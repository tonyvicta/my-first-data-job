# Who comes back?

**Tool:** SQL · **From:** Alex Morgan, my manager at Larkfield Homeware Co. (fictional)

## The request
> Nice work on the deliveries. Hannah Clarke, our Head of Customer Marketing, saw it and has asked for you.
>
> She's planning a welcome offer for new customers and wants to know what brings people back. Specifically: how many customers come back for a second order, where they live, and what they bought first.
>
> You'll need two tables: customers (everyone with an account) and orders (every order since January 2025), joined on customer_id. Careful: not every customer has ordered, so think about which number you're counting.
>
> Send me the figures, which recommendation the data supports, and a short note for Hannah. Flag anything you're unsure about.

## What I found
- **How many customers have placed at least one order?** 467 customers
- **What share of those customers have placed two or more orders?** 37.47%
- **Which region has the highest repeat rate?** North
- **What share of all revenue comes from repeat customers?** 60.54%%
- **Among repeat customers, which category was most often their first order?** Kitchen
- **What should Hannah build the welcome offer around?** A Kitchen offer: customers whose first order is Kitchen come back far more often.

## My summary for the team
Kitchen first orders bring customers back: it is the most common first purchase among repeat customers, and North has the best repeat rate (51%).
